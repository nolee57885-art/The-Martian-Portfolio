02 / Control, then express

Make Energy Useful

Strength is not only what you can produce. It is how deliberately you absorb, redirect, and release it.

II.
Translation

From potential to purposeful action.

Once a foundation is in place, the work becomes more nuanced: noticing how force is absorbed, where movement leaks, and which small changes make a task more efficient. Power matters—but so does the control that precedes it.

01

Learn to absorb

Eccentric work trains the ability to control a muscle while it lengthens under load. It can help develop braking capacity, load tolerance, and control through a movement—important qualities when decelerating, landing, or changing direction.

02

Notice the small things

Movement tracking and careful observation help bring attention to details that are easy to miss: alignment, tempo, balance, range, foot placement, trunk position, and the way a movement changes with fatigue or task demands.

03

Refine the mechanics

Sometimes progress starts with a small adjustment to technique, form, timing, or setup. The goal is not to make every movement look identical, but to find a strategy suited to the person, the task, and the intended outcome.

04

Express force

When appropriate foundations and control are established, training may progress toward faster intent, explosive actions, and plyometric tasks. These are introduced progressively, with attention to landing quality, recovery, and readiness.

05

Keep balance in the system

Balance and posture are not frozen poses. They are ongoing negotiations between the body, the task, and the environment. Training explores how to organise the body while reaching, reacting, shifting, landing, and moving.

Power is more than an output. It is the ability to meet a demand—and remain in control.

Potential energy has a precise meaning in physics; in training, the page uses it as a broader metaphor for capacity that can be developed and directed. Exercise selection and progression should be individualised.
