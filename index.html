01 / Clinical practice

Skill lives in the details.

Across seven years of daily clinical practice, I have worked with 500+ cases spanning musculoskeletal (MSK), cardiorespiratory, women’s health, and ergonomic concerns. My experience also includes paediatric and neurological presentations, including Down syndrome and cerebral palsy.

Clinical work has meant assessing people day after day, adapting approaches to the problem in front of me, and looking for practical solutions when a standard answer is not enough. I have documented patient progress through outcome photographs and explored different ways to address individual challenges. Manual therapy, soft-tissue techniques, and ischaemic compression are part of my hands-on toolkit; clinical judgement lies in deciding what may be appropriate for each person.

04 / Sport-specific thinking

Movement has a context.

Sport-specific work means looking beyond a symptom to the demands of the activity: its movement patterns, loads, skills, and the person's goals. The aim is to keep the individual, not just the injury, at the centre of the process.

05 / Building something of my own

Building a business independently asks for a different kind of endurance: planning, making decisions with incomplete information, learning from missteps, and returning to the work. It is another practice in turning energy into direction.
